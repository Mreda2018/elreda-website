The 30-second summary

  • A good logo reads in every situation: small on an icon, large on a sign, and in one colour on a stamp.
  • The 5 mistakes that weaken a logo most: too much detail, too many colours, hard-to-read type, a single version, and receiving only a JPG file.
  • Before approving any logo, test it at 24 pixels, in one colour, and from 5 metres away.
  • Do not accept delivery without a vector file (AI, SVG or PDF) and versions for light and dark backgrounds.

Why is the logo the most important decision in your visual identity?

The logo is the one element that appears almost everywhere: the business card, the invoice, your Instagram profile picture, the sign, the vehicle, the stamp and the website. Any weakness in it follows you to all these places for years, and changing it once it has spread is costly, because you reprint everything and reintroduce your brand to people.

The good news is that most logo problems are predictable and can be caught before approval. These are the five mistakes we see most in the logos clients send us to redesign.

Mistake 1: a logo full of detail

A logo that looks great on a big screen can turn into an unreadable blob when it shrinks. Shadows, many gradients, thin lines and fine drawings all disappear in a social media profile picture, a browser icon or a promotional pen.

How to test it: shrink the logo to 24 pixels (a small icon) and to 1 cm in print. If you do not recognise it immediately, it needs simplifying. Strong logos rely on one clear shape that can be drawn from memory.

Mistake 2: too many colours

Every extra colour means higher cost in some print methods (such as screen printing and embroidery), more difficulty keeping colours consistent between print and screens, and weaker recall for customers. The brands people remember are usually tied to one or two colours.

The rule: one main colour + one supporting colour + one neutral (white, black or grey). Three colours at most. Ask the designer for every colour's CMYK code for print and HEX code for screens, so the colours come out the same everywhere.

Mistake 3: hard-to-read type

Ornate, intertwined lettering can look distinctive, but if people misread your name or cannot read it from a distance on a sign or a vehicle, the ad has lost its main job. This is very common with decorative Arabic calligraphy where letters interlock.

How to test it: print the name at the size of a small sign and stand 5 metres away, or show it for two seconds to someone who does not know your business name and ask them to read it. If they hesitate, the lettering needs work.

Mistake 4: only one version of the logo

This is the mistake most business owners make. The logo will sit on light backgrounds, dark backgrounds and coloured photos, and will sometimes be printed in one colour on a stamp, invoice or bag. A single version cannot work in all of these, so the logo disappears on dark backgrounds or breaks when converted to black and white.

Mistake 5: receiving a JPG and nothing else

JPG and PNG are raster files, made of a fixed number of pixels. When you enlarge them for a roll-up or a sign, the edges look jagged and blurry. Many business owners discover this years later, when they need a large sign and cannot find the original file from the designer.

The fix: you must receive a vector file that can be enlarged to any size without losing quality.

Checklist before approving your logo

  • Clear and recognisable at 24 pixels and at 1 cm in print.
  • Works in one colour (black only and white only) without losing its shape.
  • Three colours at most, each with a CMYK and HEX code.
  • The name is readable from 5 metres.
  • There is a version for light backgrounds, one for dark, and a standalone symbol.
  • It does not resemble a well-known competitor's logo in your field.
  • You received vector files (AI, SVG or PDF) plus a transparent PNG.
  • There is a simple usage guide: clear space around the logo, minimum size, and forbidden uses.

Frequently asked questions

Can I design my logo myself or with AI?

AI tools are useful for generating first ideas, but their output is usually raster images (not vector), may contain distorted text, and may resemble existing work. Use them for inspiration, then have a designer turn the idea into a clean vector logo with a full set of versions.

What is the difference between a logo and a visual identity?

The logo is part of the identity. A visual identity covers the logo in all its versions, the colours, the typefaces, and how print and social media are designed, so everything about your brand looks consistent.

How many colours should a logo have?

One or two main colours are enough for most brands, plus a neutral. The fewer the colours, the easier the printing and the better people remember the brand.

Do I need to change my old logo?

Not necessarily. Sometimes a "refresh" is enough: simplifying the logo and preparing correct versions of it without changing the shape people know. Test it with the checklist above and decide based on the result.

Which files should I receive from the designer?

An editable source file (AI or EPS), SVG, vector PDF and transparent PNG for every version of the logo (primary, reversed, single colour and the symbol alone), together with the colour codes and typefaces used.
