The 30-second summary

  • A successful online store in Egypt = the right platform + payment methods customers trust + reliable shipping + clear product content. Design alone does not sell.
  • Egyptian customers expect cash on delivery alongside cards (Visa, Mastercard and Meeza), mobile wallets and Fawry.
  • You have 3 routes: a hosted platform on a subscription, WordPress with WooCommerce, or a custom-built store. The choice depends on your catalogue size, budget and growth plans.
  • Before launch, prepare: a commercial register and tax card (to activate payment gateways), a shipping and returns policy, and good photos and descriptions for every product.

Do you need an online store, or is selling on social media enough?

Selling through messages on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp is a good way to start, but as orders grow its problems show: the same questions about prices and sizes get asked hundreds of times, orders get lost in chats, stock is hard to track, and there is no organised way to take online payments. Most importantly, your social media audience belongs to the platform, not to you.

A store solves these problems. Customers see the price, sizes and photos, and order and pay on their own at any time, while you receive a complete order with the address and phone number. Social media stays the source of traffic that sends people to the store.

Which payment methods should your store support in Egypt?

  • Cash on delivery (COD): still the most used, and dropping it loses you a large share of customers, especially on a first order.
  • Bank cards: Visa, Mastercard and Meeza, through a local payment gateway.
  • Mobile wallets: such as Vodafone Cash and others.
  • Payment through Fawry: a reference code the customer pays in cash at the nearest Fawry outlet.
  • Instalments: through instalment providers, useful for higher-priced products.

Local payment gateways such as Paymob, Fawry and Kashier bring most of these methods together in one integration. To activate any gateway you will usually need a commercial register, a tax card and a bank account in the business's name. Compare gateways on the fee per transaction, how quickly money reaches your account, and how easily they connect to your platform.

How should you organise shipping and returns?

  • A local courier that supports cash collection on delivery and covers the governorates you sell in, with a dashboard to track shipments.
  • Clear shipping prices at checkout by governorate. A surprise shipping fee at the last step is a major reason people abandon their cart.
  • A written returns and exchange policy in the store: the time limit, the conditions, and who pays for return shipping. It builds trust on a first order.
  • Order confirmation by WhatsApp message or call before shipping, to cut orders refused at the door.

Store launch checklist

  • A domain in your business name (.com or .com.eg) and a business email.
  • Clear product photos on a consistent background, at least 3 per product.
  • A description for each product that answers customers' questions: sizes, material, use and warranty.
  • A design that loads fast on mobile, since most orders come from it.
  • Payment methods activated and tested with a real order.
  • Pages for About, Contact, Shipping and Returns, and Privacy.
  • A visible WhatsApp button for quick questions.
  • Measurement tools connected (Google Analytics and the Meta pixel) before any paid ads.
  • Basic search engine setup: a title and description for each product, clean URLs, and a sitemap.

Common mistakes that kill sales

  • Product photos from suppliers or the internet: the same photo appears on ten competitors' stores, so customers see no reason to buy from you.
  • Too many checkout steps: forcing customers to create an account before buying, or asking for unnecessary details.
  • A slow store on mobile: every second of delay means customers closing the page before it loads.
  • Launching ads before testing the whole purchase journey: you pay for visits to a store with a broken payment or shipping step.
  • Keeping the store separate from stock and accounts: you sell a product that is out of stock, or enter every order into the accounts by hand.

Why connect your store to an accounting system?

As orders grow, copying each order by hand into accounts and inventory becomes a constant source of errors. A mature store connects to an accounting system, so stock is deducted automatically with every sale, the invoice or e-receipt required for tax is issued, and you see your real profit after shipping and fees. Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to build an online store?

It depends on the number of products, how ready the photos and content are, and how the store is built. The part that usually takes time is not the development, but preparing product photos and descriptions and activating the payment gateway.

Do I need a commercial register to open an online store?

To activate online payment gateways and issue official invoices you will usually need a commercial register and a tax card. You can start with cash on delivery while you complete the paperwork.

Shopify, WordPress or a custom store?

Shopify for a quick start with no technical management, WordPress with WooCommerce for more control at a reasonable cost, and a custom store when you have special needs or sales volume that justifies the investment.

Should I offer cash on delivery?

In the Egyptian market: yes, at least at the start. Many customers will not pay online on a first order from a new store, and once they trust you some of them switch to online payment.

How do I get the store's first customers?

Start with your existing customers on social media and WhatsApp, then small targeted paid campaigns once the purchase journey is tested. Alongside that, publish useful content and search-optimised products so free traffic builds over time.
