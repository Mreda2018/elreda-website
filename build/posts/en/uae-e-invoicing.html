The 30-second summary

  • E-invoicing in the UAE becomes mandatory in phases under Ministerial Decision No. 244 of 2025 and its amendments, and covers invoices between businesses (B2B) and with government entities (B2G).
  • Businesses with annual revenue of AED 50 million or more: the deadline to appoint an Accredited Service Provider (ASP) was 30 October 2026, and mandatory e-invoicing starts on 1 January 2027.
  • All other in-scope businesses: appoint an ASP by 31 March 2027 and go live on 1 July 2027. Government entities are expected to start in October 2027.
  • Invoices are sent in the PINT AE format through an accredited service provider, and their tax data is reported to the Federal Tax Authority. You cannot send them directly without an ASP.

What is e-invoicing in the UAE?

It is a system in which businesses issue invoices as structured data (not PDF or paper). The invoice travels electronically from the seller's system to the buyer's system, and at the same time its tax data is reported to the Federal Tax Authority (FTA). The programme is run by the Ministry of Finance, and it differs from Egypt's system in one key way: in the UAE a business does not send its invoice to the tax authority itself. It goes through an Accredited Service Provider (ASP).

How does the 5-corner model work?

The UAE adopted what is known as the five-corner model. In short:

  1. The seller (corner 1)

    Issues the invoice from its accounting system with PINT AE data.

  2. The seller's service provider (corner 2)

    Validates the invoice, converts it to the required format if needed, and sends it on.

  3. The buyer's service provider (corner 3)

    Receives the invoice and delivers it to the buyer's system.

  4. The buyer (corner 4)

    Receives the invoice straight into its accounting system, with no manual entry.

  5. The Federal Tax Authority (corner 5)

    Receives the invoice's tax data once validation succeeds. If validation fails, a rejection status goes back to the seller and no tax data is reported.

PINT AE is the UAE version of the international Peppol invoice specification, which is why UAE invoices can also be exchanged with companies outside the country on the same network.

The Ministry of Finance extended the ASP deadline for large businesses from 31 July to 30 October 2026, and confirmed that the January 2027 go-live date did not change. The lesson: the ASP deadline may move, but the go-live date holds, so do not wait for the last minute.

Does this apply to my business?

  • The system covers B2B (business to business) and B2G (business to government) transactions.
  • Sales to end consumers (B2C) are not mandated for now, so a business that sells only to individuals is not in scope at this stage.
  • What matters is your revenue and the type of transactions. Check your last financial year's revenue to know your phase.
Important: the Ministerial Decisions set out the detailed scope and exclusions, and they are amended regularly. Check your company's position on the UAE Ministry of Finance website or with your tax adviser.

How to prepare before your deadline

  1. Identify your phase

    From last year's revenue, work out your ASP deadline and go-live date.

  2. Review your accounting system

    Can it issue invoices with all the mandatory PINT AE fields? Or are you still invoicing from Excel or Word? This is the biggest change in the whole project.

  3. Clean your data

    Customers' and suppliers' Tax Registration Numbers (TRN), correct legal names, and item and tax classifications. Bad data is the number one reason invoices get rejected.

  4. Choose an accredited service provider

    From the Ministry's list of accredited providers. Compare them on ease of integration with your system, pricing by invoice volume, and technical support.

  5. Connect and test

    Connect your accounting system to the provider, send test invoices, and make sure you receive acceptance and rejection statuses.

  6. Train your team

    On issuing invoices and credit notes, and on handling rejected invoices, before your go-live date.

Are there penalties?

Yes. A Cabinet Decision sets administrative fines for e-invoicing violations. They include fines for not implementing the system or not appointing a service provider on time, for late issue and transmission of invoices and credit notes, and for late notice to the Authority of a system failure. The amounts are set by the decisions in force, so check them on the Ministry's official website.

Frequently asked questions

When does mandatory e-invoicing start in the UAE?

On 1 January 2027 for businesses with annual revenue of AED 50 million or more, and on 1 July 2027 for all other in-scope businesses. Government entities are expected to start in October 2027.

What is an Accredited Service Provider (ASP)?

A provider accredited by the Ministry of Finance that validates your invoices, sends them to the buyer's provider, and reports their data to the Federal Tax Authority. A business cannot send invoices itself, and must appoint an accredited provider before its phase's deadline.

Do I need to change my accounting system?

Not necessarily. What matters is that your system can issue invoices with PINT AE data and connect to a service provider. If you rely on Excel or software that cannot do this, you will need a new system or an intermediate solution.

Are sales to individuals (B2C) included?

No, sales to end consumers are not mandated at this stage. But if you also sell to businesses, those invoices are in scope.

What does PINT AE mean?

It is the UAE e-invoice specification, built on the international Peppol standard. It defines the mandatory fields and how the invoice data is structured.

My company operates in Egypt and the UAE. Do I need two systems?

You can use one system that supports the rules of both countries, such as Faktra, instead of running two separate systems.
